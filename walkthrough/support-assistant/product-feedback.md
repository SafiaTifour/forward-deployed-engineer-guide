# Product feedback loop

This page is essential because it shows how a local problem can become a reusable product improvement.

## Hypothetical examples
- Repeated permission errors become a reusable authorization integration.
- Confusing policy versions become shared document-version handling.
- Inconsistent reviewer feedback becomes a reusable evaluation rubric.

## When to keep it local
A local customization should remain local when it is tightly bound to a customer’s process, compliance requirements, or a rare workflow that is not broadly generalizable.

## When to generalize
A pattern should be generalized when it addresses a recurring need across multiple workflows or customers, especially when the underlying issue is structural rather than merely local.

## Final principle
The product feedback loop is not about collecting every customer complaint into one giant product. It is about recognizing which patterns are both operationally relevant and reusable enough to justify product work.
