# 02. FDE vs other engineering roles

It is tempting to treat titles as rigid categories. In practice, engineering roles overlap substantially. The most useful way to compare them is by typical emphasis rather than by strict boundaries.

## Software engineer

A software engineer typically focuses on building and maintaining software systems, often within a product or platform team. The emphasis is usually on code quality, abstraction, architecture, reliability, and maintainability.

Typical emphasis:
- product implementation
- system design
- maintainability
- code quality
- feature delivery

A software engineer may speak to customers, but it is not necessarily the defining part of the role.

## AI engineer

An AI engineer often works on applied AI systems: model integration, prompt design, evaluation, orchestration, retrieval, data pipelines, and deployment standards for AI features. The common emphasis is on making AI components work reliably within a product or internal workflow.

Typical emphasis:
- model integration
- prompt or tool design
- retrieval and evaluation
- experimentation
- operationalizing AI features

This role overlaps with FDE work, especially in AI deployments. The key distinction is that AI engineer is often a more technology-centric description, whereas FDE more often emphasizes proximate customer context and deployment learning.

## Solutions engineer

A solutions engineer typically works on customer fit, technical discovery, architecture alignment, and explaining how a product or platform can address a customer need. The role often includes demos, workflow discussion, and technical coordination across stakeholders.

Typical emphasis:
- product fit
- customer discovery
- architecture conversations
- demonstrations and technical scoping
- adoption support

This role overlaps with FDE work, but it should not be caricatured as “only demos.” High-quality solutions engineering often includes understanding customer workflows and constraints, which is precisely the sort of work that can feed product and engineering decisions. The difference is that FDE work often includes a stronger implementation and delivery posture, not just technical explanation or solution framing.

## Forward deployed engineer

A Forward Deployed Engineer usually sits closer to the deployment and operational reality than a traditional software or AI engineer. The role often includes working with customers or internal stakeholders to understand a real pain point, implement a workable system, evaluate it, and then decide what part of the solution should become reusable product capability.

Typical emphasis:
- customer proximity
- scope and prioritization
- implementation in context
- workflow design
- learning loop back into product
- operational reliability and institutional constraints

This definition is intentionally broad because the role varies by employer. The common pattern is customer-facing engineering with strong delivery ownership.

## Overlap and title variation

The boundaries are porous. Many teams blend the responsibilities of solutions engineering, applied AI engineering, and product engineering. A title alone is not enough to determine the work. The more informative question is: what are the expected responsibilities and how much customer-context work is part of the role?

The repository’s synthesis is that the most meaningful contrast is not “which role is best,” but what kind of problem the job is built to solve and how close it is to the actual deployment environment.

## Why this distinction matters

An employer may use the term FDE for work that has a strong platform edge, while another employer may use a different title for similar work. The guide avoids claiming that titles mean the same thing everywhere. It instead emphasizes the recurring pattern: working close to real problems, delivering something useful, and learning from the deployment back into the product.
